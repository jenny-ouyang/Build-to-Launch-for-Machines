# Substack Growth Hub: Everything I've Learned About Growing on Substack —Notes, Niche, and 10x Growth Patterns

**Track:** Distribution Hub
**Published:** 2026-04-09

## Summary

This is the chronological hub for everything Jenny has learned growing on Substack, written so a reader can find the stage they're at and start there. The frame is honest about the starting point: zero audience, zero niche, no clear plan, while holding a full-time job and raising two toddlers. By the time of writing the same account had passed 6,000 subscribers and 200 paid. Rather than a list of tactics, the page walks the actual order things happened, attaching the article written at each stage to the moment it was relevant.

The early sections cover the questions every new writer faces: whether there was a niche worth growing into, and how to answer that with data instead of guesswork. That leads into building a Substack niche analyzer — a tool the reader can rebuild — and the lived experience of the first four months, when growth feels slow and the signal is hard to read. The first paid launch gets its own section, including what it actually felt like to charge for the first time.

From there the hub moves into the patterns that compounded. It looks at what 3,000+ articles by top AI writers reveal about what spreads, what the Substack Rising Badge does and does not mean, and the viral Substack Notes system built on studying what works. The growth arc is told in milestones — crossing 4,000 subscribers in year one, then reaching roughly 4,500, and where the account stands now at 6,000 subscribers and 200 paid.

The closing sections turn the journey into something usable for someone who skipped it: how to grow a Substack with Claude, what 3,000 Notes from nine creators show (and the MCP built to study them), and branching paths depending on whether the reader wants the full zero-to-now playbook, help finding a niche, or a Notes-first growth approach. It cross-links the Claude Hub and positions itself against generic Substack advice by being tested on one real newsletter rather than borrowed.
