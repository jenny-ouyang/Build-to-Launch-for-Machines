# How I Built an SEO System That Tells Me What to Change Next
**URL:** https://buildtolaunch.substack.com/p/automated-seo-monitoring-claude-ai
**Track:** Distribution Hub
**Published:** 2026-08-25

## Summary

Jenny's starting claim is that dashboards show what happened, but handing the raw Search Console and Analytics data to Claude lets her ask what to do about it. She walks through a series of real questions and their answers to prove the difference. Asked which pages need attention, Claude sorted her Search Console pages into act-now, watch, and leave-alone buckets, and caught a case where a page that looked like the bigger opportunity over a 28-day window had actually already collapsed in more recent traffic — acting on the older number would have meant fixing a page after the audience had already left. Asked whether her own pages were competing with each other, Claude found that out of 11,473 queries, 347 returned two or more of her own pages, and those cannibalized searches converted at a lower click-through rate than searches where only one of her pages appeared; the clearest case was three separate articles all ranking for "claude cowork," together pulling almost 5,000 impressions and zero clicks.

The analysis got broader from there. Comparing referral traffic across her publication and her product sites showed her which articles were actually sending readers onward and which products were being discovered through search versus AI assistants — in one comparison, ChatGPT referrals outpaced Google by roughly four to one. A dated check of search results surfaced a competitor publishing similar Claude Code content at a much faster cadence, giving her a plausible explanation for a ranking shift that her own performance numbers alone couldn't account for. The most consequential question crossed her backlog of article ideas against actual search demand: out of dozens of shortlisted topics, Claude recommended a specific how-to piece first because the related search terms were already generating substantial impressions that her existing page on the topic wasn't converting, while also telling her which ideas to drop because there simply wasn't demand behind them, or because the demand was already being served.

Once the ad hoc questions proved useful, Jenny turned them into a recurring job inside the scheduled-task "second brain" system she'd already built for other automations. The weekly run pulls fresh Search Console, Analytics, and Bing data, checks that all the connections are still healthy, and produces one dated report that always ends in one of four calls: improve an existing page, create a page that's missing, change the underlying product or message based on observed demand, or leave things alone and keep measuring. Optionally, it cross-references her separate research-collection automation so that topics surfacing in her reading get checked against search demand before she decides whether they're worth writing about.

The setup portion of the article is the paid deliverable, and it's substantial: connecting a single site to both Search Console and GA4 in the browser first (choosing between a domain-level or URL-prefix property, installing the GA4 measurement tag, and proving the pipeline with a controlled Realtime pageview test) before layering in a read-only Google Cloud service account so a downloaded automation packet can pull data programmatically instead of through manual exports. That setup walks through creating a Cloud project, enabling the two relevant APIs, generating a service-account key with no broader project role, granting that account Restricted access in Search Console and Viewer access in GA4, and verifying the whole chain with a connection check before running a command that saves paired 7-day and 28-day snapshots from both sources to disk. From there the packet exposes a bank of question categories — what moved and why, which pages are competing with themselves, how content maps to product outcomes, and who the real search competitors are — plus the ideas-crossing prompt, which is deliberately constrained never to invent an idea that wasn't already on her list and to check for self-cannibalization before recommending a new page on a topic she already covers.

The article closes by explicitly walking back the instinct to automate immediately. Jenny recommends starting with two manual CSV exports and one question before connecting any API, since every finding she describes earlier in the piece came out of that manual route first, and warns that scheduling an unproven pipeline just produces confidently wrong answers on a fixed cadence rather than correct ones. The scaling section covers what changes once several properties are registered — her own setup spans six, including her publication, product sites, and a personal domain — which unlocks cross-property questions no single Search Console view can answer, alongside four specific failure modes she hit while scaling: averaging metrics across genuinely separate businesses, mistaking an unmeasured site for a dead one, comparing properties of very different ages as if they were peers, and assuming a data source is automatable just because it's visible in an interface (social platforms inside Search Console, in her testing, can't yet be pulled automatically and have to be tracked as manual, dated exports instead).
