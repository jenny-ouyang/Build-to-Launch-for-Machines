# Make Your AI the Partner You Build With (Introducing the new Build to Launch MCP)
**URL:** https://buildtolaunch.substack.com/p/claude-mcp-build-to-launch
**Track:** Products Built and Shipped
**Published:** 2026-11-18

## Summary

The article introduces the current state of the Build to Launch MCP, a remote connector that puts Jenny's entire published workflow library in front of any AI app that supports the Model Context Protocol, including Claude, ChatGPT, Codex, Gemini CLI and Cursor. Started in April as a small server holding a handful of collected workflows, it has grown into two linked halves: a library of 475 items (155 workflows, 107 guides, 81 setups, 72 prompts and 60 examples) organized into nine job-shaped "doors" covering research, writing, image and video work, building, automation, selling and growth, and maintenance; and a set of 143 "buddies," each one a connector to a category of public data such as Substack, SEO tools, web search, job boards, Instagram, YouTube and Reddit. A single front-door tool, Ask BTL, sits above both halves and takes a plain-language job description, picks the right guide, workflow or buddy on its own, and shows its plan before running anything, so a user never has to know the library's internal names.

To show rather than describe the system, the piece walks through a connected chain of seven jobs run against a single hypothetical product, a pruning-focused backyard garden app. The idea-validation step runs competitive, demand and sentiment checks before any code is written and comes back recommending a pivot: the originally described app already exists, but search demand for narrow intent terms like "when to prune" outweighs demand for generic "garden planner" phrasing, so the piece returns three alternative angles to test instead of a straight go/no-go. The following step turns the two incumbent apps the research surfaced into full business teardowns, with the AI making roughly 17 independent calls to pull pricing pages, ad libraries and search-referral data, filtering out a YouTube channel that didn't actually belong to either brand, and concluding that neither competitor owns the narrow "prune now" moment the new idea could claim. A third job sets up a recurring Sunday digest of gardening newsletters and YouTube channels, scored by a consistency tool called Jev so the same numeric score means the same thing week over week, with a test run shown before the real schedule fires.

The design and content examples follow the same build-then-launch arc. A first app screen gets built, then independently scored by a second model acting as reviewer, which found 27 of 40 points and eight issues before all eight were fixed, catching a mobile layout problem the builder itself had missed; a companion printable pruning calendar is produced by reading eleven university extension pages rather than inventing dates. On the content side, a keyword-intent check before writing an unrelated explainer article found search volume around 18,100 monthly U.S. searches for the plain-language phrasing and roughly four times the interest of two years earlier, skewed toward beginners, which shaped both the decision to write it and the exact wording to target. A separate pass checking an AI-written draft section for "AI flavor" found four tells, fixed three automatically and flagged one for the writer's own judgment, with most of the issues traced to a single habit of short declarative beats used for forced rhythm rather than real emphasis.

The closing example is a launch-readiness and discoverability check run against a real site, vibecoding.builders, which read the live code without changing anything, checked three competing sites, and returned a mixed verdict: the site was ready for more traffic pending three fixes, but it ranked for only five search terms, its AI-crawler access was wide open, and it hadn't yet been cited in AI-generated search summaries, so "ready to ship" and "ready to be found" turned out to be two different questions with two different answers. The article then prices the same seven jobs against buying the underlying categories separately — SEO tools, ad-intelligence tools, scraping services, freelance research and writing help — landing in the $260–600-per-month range before any hourly labor, against a membership price framed as $112 for the first year at a 30%-off window closing November 30, rising to $160 afterward. It closes with three connection paths (pasting an install instruction into a terminal-based coding agent, adding it as a custom connector inside Claude, or adding it as a custom MCP server inside ChatGPT's plugin settings) and a stated division of labor: the mechanical steps — searching, scoring, cross-checking, catching a wrong data point — are handed to the AI, while choosing what's worth building, who it's for, and when it's ready to ship stays with the person running it.
