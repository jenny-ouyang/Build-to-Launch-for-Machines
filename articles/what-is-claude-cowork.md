## Summary

Claude Cowork launched in January 2026 as a separate mode inside Claude Desktop. It isn't an upgrade to chat — it's a different way of working with Claude: hand it a multi-step task, walk away, and come back to a finished file on your computer. This guide explains what Cowork actually is, how it works under the hood, what the equivalents from ChatGPT and Gemini look like, and who the tool is and isn't built for.

The opening defines Cowork and then gets into mechanics: the task flow, how work is dispatched, and how scheduled tasks let Cowork run on its own without you sitting in the loop. That under-the-hood view is what separates Cowork from a chat window — the unit of work is a delegated task with a deliverable, not a turn-by-turn conversation.

To make it concrete, the article suggests three Cowork tasks worth starting in the first week, then sets Cowork against the alternatives. It compares it to ChatGPT, Gemini, and Microsoft 365 so a reader can see where it overlaps with tools they already pay for and where it doesn't, and it breaks down who should use Cowork by role rather than treating it as one-size-fits-all.

The most useful section is the decision guidance: when to use Cowork versus Claude chat versus Claude Code, since the three serve different shapes of work. The piece ends honestly with the limitations most guides leave out, a next-steps path, and an FAQ — so the reader can decide whether Cowork fits their actual workflow before committing to it.
