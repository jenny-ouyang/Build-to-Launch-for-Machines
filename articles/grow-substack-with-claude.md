# How to Grow Your Substack With Limited Time and Zero Social Energy
**URL:** https://buildtolaunch.substack.com/p/grow-substack-with-claude
**Track:** Distribution Hub
**Published:** 2026-04-22

## Summary

Jenny opens against the standard Substack growth advice — engage constantly, write notes, exchange recommendations — by pointing out that all of it assumes spare time and social energy she genuinely didn't have: a full-time job, two toddlers, and roughly four spare hours a day split across everything. Her claim is that she still reached over 6,000 subscribers and 200-plus paid members in twelve months, not by discovering some algorithmic trick but by understanding precisely what each level of the standard growth advice actually buys you, and then building tools to do the slow parts of each one faster. The article is explicitly two things stacked together: the underlying three-level system anyone can run manually for free, and a Claude-powered "fast track" for paid subscribers that removes the friction from all three without adding to the time budget.

The free section lays out what each level is actually for, since she argues most writers stall by over-investing in one and skipping the others. Level 1, recommendation exchanges, is about surface area — putting your newsletter in front of an audience that already cares about adjacent topics — and she illustrates its leverage and its limits with her own experience: her best early exchange brought in more than 40 new subscribers in two days (an asymmetric win the other, larger writer wasn't thrilled about), but she's also seen writers with hundreds of exchanges stuck around 2,000 subscribers because they picked the wrong partners or over-optimized the mechanism against themselves — her rule is to prioritize topical fit and evidence of active, recent, still-growing publishing over raw subscriber count, and to treat exchanges as a support system rather than the product itself. Level 2, notes, is about staying visible between full articles, and her research into the space is concrete: she ran roughly 3,000 notes from nine admired creators through Claude and found that the single most-posted format across all of them was actually the worst performer — each creator's best-converting notes followed patterns specific to that person, not a universal formula, which pushed her toward publishing consistently, tracking what actually converts to subscribers rather than just likes, and favoring specific, lived detail (real numbers, real friction, something she genuinely tried) over generic insight. Level 3, articles, is the compounding layer, and she's candid about getting it wrong for a long stretch herself — writing strong standalone pieces on related technical topics without ever linking them together or revisiting them, missing the internal web that lets older work keep pulling in readers months later; what separates writers whose content compounds is consistent voice, deliberate internal backlinking, headings that match what people actually search for, and pieces built to stand alone while still reinforcing each other.

The paid "fast track" doesn't introduce a different system — it wires Claude into three tools she built herself to remove the time cost from each level. For Level 1, Substack Explorer (her own web app, connected to Claude via MCP) searches a database of over 100,000 newsletters, filtered by niche, subscriber range, and recency, to surface a shortlist of exchange candidates in minutes, and Claude then drafts a personalized outreach DM referencing something specific about the target newsletter rather than a generic pitch; she reports roughly half of these outreach attempts converting to a yes, and notes as a practical aside that Claude's Chat or Code modes work better for this than Cowork, which tends to over-analyze rather than just return candidates. For Level 2, a combination of her QuickViralNotes tool, a companion Chrome scheduling extension, and a Substack MCP server extracts note-worthy passages directly out of her own published articles — solving what used to be a recurring half-hour of manually rereading and reformatting — and can also surface her own historically best-performing note formats when she's out of ideas, then batch-schedule a week's worth of notes with spacing rules and up to six attached links per note for carousel-style posts. For Level 3, no new tool is needed at all: the same Substack MCP handles reading her inbox in one prompt, researching what other newsletters she follows have said about a given topic, pulling her own recent article performance metrics, and retrieving the full text of paid articles she's subscribed to, all without switching tabs.

She's explicit about what these tools do and don't do: they don't grow the newsletter on their own, they remove friction at the specific points where limited time causes a task to simply not get done — an hour of manual partner research collapses into a ten-minute prompt, staring at a blank note draft becomes an extraction-and-scheduling pass over material she already wrote, and a scattered four- or five-tab research-and-performance check becomes one prompt. The piece closes with a bonus "prompt pack" for paid subscribers — a DM template for recommendation-exchange outreach (both a fill-in-the-blanks manual version and a version Claude personalizes automatically once a candidate is chosen) plus a set of note-focused prompts covering pulling fresh note candidates out of a new article, surfacing inspiration from her own top-performing past notes, batch-scheduling a week of notes at once, and studying another creator's posting patterns to adapt rather than copy — framed as a five-prompt, once-a-week routine that keeps the whole three-level system running on very little of her own time.
